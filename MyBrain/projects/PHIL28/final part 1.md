### Part I · Q1 — Fish（约 250 词）

> 题目：批判性评估 Fish 的主张——"言论自由只是我们给那种服务于我们想推进的实质性议程的言语行为所起的名字。"

**英文成稿：**

Fish's claim sounds shocking, but it is easy to misread. He is not saying free speech is worthless or that everyone is selfish. He is making two points.

First, "free speech" has no fixed content of its own. It is like an empty label. People fill it with whatever cause they care about, and then call that cause "free speech" because the label wins respect. So the label is a prize that each side wants to claim.

Second, because the label is empty, the law cannot use it as a neutral rule. To apply it, a court must draw a line between protected and unprotected speech. But every line favors one side over another. So the line is always a political choice that only pretends to be neutral.

Is he right? On the main point, yes. There really is no "pure" speech with no effects, so any rule must choose which speech to protect, and that choice is never neutral. This is a sharp and honest insight.

But his claim has a weak spot. If every standard is just politics, then his own view is also just politics. His judgment that we should now regulate hate speech rests on no firmer ground than the views he attacks. He admits this himself. So his claim explains a great deal, but it gives us little guidance on what we should actually do.

**中文对照：**

Fish 的主张听起来惊人，但很容易被误读。他不是说言论自由没用，也不是说人人自私。他在讲两个点。

第一，"言论自由"本身没有固定内容，像个空标签。人们想推进什么事业，就往里填什么，然后把那个事业叫"言论自由"，因为这个标签能赢得尊重。所以这个标签是各方争着去抢的奖品。

第二，正因为标签是空的，法律没法把它当成一条中立规则。要应用它，法院必须在"受保护"和"不受保护"的言论之间划线。但每条线都偏袒一方。所以那条线永远是个政治选择，只是假装中立。

他对吗？在主要点上，对。确实不存在"无后果的纯言论"，所以任何规则都得选择保护哪些言论，而这个选择从来不中立。这是个犀利而诚实的洞察。

但他的主张有个软肋。如果一切标准都只是政治，那他自己的观点也只是政治。他"现在应当管制仇恨言论"的判断，并不比他所攻击的观点站在更稳的地基上。他自己也承认这点。所以他的主张解释力很强，却几乎给不出"我们究竟该怎么做"的指引。

### Part I · Q10 — Shiffrin（约 250 词）

> 题目：Shiffrin 主张商业言论不同于个人言论、可能理应受到更少的、免于监管的保护——批判性评估她的理由。

**英文成稿：**

Shiffrin argues that business and commercial speech can get weaker protection than individual speech. Her reasons follow from her main theory: speech matters most because it lets a thinking person form and share the contents of their mind.

She gives several reasons. First, corporate speech does not really reveal any person's inner thoughts. Its goal is often to sell, not to share a mind. Second, it comes from a setting built around profit, which pushes the firm to say whatever helps business, not what it sincerely believes. So we cannot assume such speech is honest or freely chosen. Third, a firm is not really a person, and its freedom is not valuable in itself. Firms also control money and resources, so society has a fair interest in regulating them.

Her reasons are mostly careful and convincing. She does not overstate them. She says "often," not "always," and she allows exceptions for businesses that pursue a moral aim. She also insists the government's reason for regulating must still be a fair one.

But there is a weak spot. Much of her argument is that market pressure shapes what the firm says. Yet individual speech is also shaped by social pressure. If outside pressure does not lower the protection of personal speech, why should it lower the protection of business speech? Her line between the two may be less clean than she suggests. Still, her core point holds: a firm is not a thinker.

**中文对照：**

Shiffrin 主张商业/公司言论可以比个人言论受更弱的保护。她的理由来自她的主理论：言论之所以最重要，是因为它让一个会思考的人形成并分享自己心灵的内容。

她给了几条理由。第一，公司言论并不真正揭示任何个人的内心想法，它的目的常常是卖货，而不是分享心灵。第二，它出自一个围绕利润搭建的环境，这种环境逼着公司说"对生意有利"的话，而不是它真诚相信的话。所以我们不能假定这种言论是诚实的、或自由选择的。第三，公司不是真正的人，它的自由本身没有价值；公司还控制着金钱和资源，所以社会对监管它们有正当利益。

她的理由大多谨慎且有说服力。她没有把话说过头：她说的是"常常"，不是"总是"，而且她为追求道德目标的企业保留了例外。她还坚持，政府管制的理由本身也必须正当。

但有个软肋。她的论证很大一部分是"市场压力塑造了公司说什么"。可是个人言论也受社会压力塑造。如果外部压力不会降低个人言论的保护，那为什么它就该降低商业言论的保护？她在两者之间划的那条线，也许没她说的那么干净。不过她的核心点站得住：公司不是一个思想者。
